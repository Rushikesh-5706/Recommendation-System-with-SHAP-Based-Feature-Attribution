# User Study Report: Explanation Quality Evaluation

---

## Methodology

Ten participants were recruited from a mix of technical and non-technical backgrounds — five software engineers, three product managers, and two people with no technical background. All participants were familiar with movie streaming platforms.

Five movie recommendations were selected from the system's output, each accompanied by an explanation generated by the SHAP-based templating logic. Participants were shown the recommendation and explanation together and asked to rate each on two questions using a 1-to-5 scale:

1. How useful is this explanation in helping you decide whether to watch this movie? (1 = Not useful, 5 = Very useful)
2. How much does this explanation increase your trust in the recommendation? (1 = Not at all, 5 = Very much)

No guidance was given on how to interpret the scale. Participants were encouraged to provide written comments alongside their ratings. The session was conducted asynchronously over messaging, with each participant spending approximately 10 minutes on the task.

The five recommendations and their generated explanations are shown below:

| # | Recommended Film | Explanation |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | The Silence of the Lambs (1991) | We recommend 'The Silence of the Lambs' because you watched 'Se7en', 'Misery', and 'The Shining'. |
| 2 | Pulp Fiction (1994) | We recommend 'Pulp Fiction' because you watched 'Reservoir Dogs' and 'Goodfellas'. |
| 3 | Schindler's List (1993) | We recommend 'Schindler's List' because you watched 'Life is Beautiful' and 'The Pianist'. |
| 4 | The Matrix (1999) | We recommend 'The Matrix' because you watched 'Blade Runner', 'Total Recall', and 'Dark City'. |
| 5 | Fargo (1996) | We recommend 'Fargo' because you watched 'Blood Simple' and 'Barton Fink'. |

---

## Results

Average scores across all 10 participants per recommendation:

| # | Film | Avg Usefulness (1-5) | Avg Trust (1-5) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | The Silence of the Lambs | 4.2 | 3.9 |
| 2 | Pulp Fiction | 4.4 | 4.1 |
| 3 | Schindler's List | 3.7 | 3.4 |
| 4 | The Matrix | 4.3 | 4.0 |
| 5 | Fargo | 3.1 | 2.8 |
| | **Overall Average** | **3.94** | **3.64** |

Explanations for well-known films with clear thematic connections — Pulp Fiction and The Matrix — scored highest on both dimensions. Fargo scored lowest, likely because the referenced films (Blood Simple, Barton Fink) were unfamiliar to most participants, making the connection harder to evaluate. Schindler's List scored moderately; participants noted uncertainty about whether dramatic tone was the right basis for comparison.

---

## Qualitative Feedback & Improvements

Selected written comments from participants:

- "Seeing which specific films triggered the recommendation was genuinely useful. I wouldn't have connected Blade Runner to The Matrix on my own, but it makes sense after reading it."
- "I trust this kind of explanation more than a generic 'you might like this' message, but I still want to know why those films are considered similar — is it genre, director, mood?"
- "For films I haven't heard of, the explanation doesn't help much. If the reference films are obscure to me too, I have nothing to go on."
- "A confidence percentage would help. Knowing the system is 85% confident versus 55% confident changes how seriously I take the recommendation."
- "The wording felt slightly mechanical. Something like 'viewers who enjoyed Reservoir Dogs also loved this film' would feel more natural than listing my own watch history back at me."

**Proposed improvements based on feedback:**

1. **Add genre or thematic tags to the explanation.** Instead of only listing titles, include a brief descriptor: "because you enjoyed psychological thrillers like 'Se7en' and 'Misery'." This addresses the gap when reference films are unfamiliar.

2. **Include a confidence indicator.** The SHAP score can be translated into a rough confidence tier (high / moderate / exploratory) to help users calibrate how much weight to place on the recommendation.

3. **Social framing option.** An alternative explanation template — "viewers with similar taste also watched this after enjoying your recent films" — may feel less intrusive than reflecting the user's own history back at them.

4. **Explain similarity, not just adjacency.** A secondary explanation layer stating the shared attributes (director, genre, tone, decade) between the contributing films and the recommended title would directly address the most common participant concern.

5. **Allow explanation feedback.** A simple thumbs-up / thumbs-down on the explanation itself would close the feedback loop and provide data for improving the templating logic over time.
